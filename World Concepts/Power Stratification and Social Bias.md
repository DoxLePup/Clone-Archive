Within modern society, Divine Gifts granted by Astraea are universally acknowledged yet unevenly interpreted. Although every humanoid possesses a single Divine Gift, cultural norms have developed that categorize these powers into perceived alignments. This classification has no divine basis and exists solely as a social construct, yet it exerts significant influence over law, reputation, and opportunity.

Society broadly divides Divine Gifts into three informal categories: Bright, Dark, and Gray.

Bright Powers are those widely viewed as beneficial, protective, or socially desirable. These include abilities associated with healing, defense, purification, or visible heroism. Individuals identified as Bright Users often receive implicit trust, social elevation, and preferential treatment. Their powers are frequently celebrated in public narratives, historical records, and institutional leadership.

Dark Powers are those associated, whether accurately or not, with fear, control, destruction, or moral ambiguity. Shadow manipulation, mental influence, decay, and similar abilities are commonly placed in this category. Dark Users are subject to heightened scrutiny, social suspicion, and, in some regions, legal restriction. This treatment persists despite the absence of inherent malice in the powers themselves. The stigma surrounding Dark Powers is largely shaped by historical misuse, rumor, and collective fear rather than consistent evidence.

Gray Powers occupy the perceived middle ground. These abilities are deeply personal, identity-focused, or internally oriented, with limited direct impact on others. They are neither widely praised nor widely feared. Gray Users are often overlooked, underestimated, or dismissed as inconsequential, despite the profound personal significance their Gifts may hold. MawLex’s gender-morphing Gift is a primary example of a Gray Power: transformative, intrinsic, and meaningful, yet not oriented toward external influence or authority.

This system of classification has given rise to a form of systemic bias commonly referred to as Giftism. Giftism manifests as prejudice, profiling, or discrimination based on the perceived alignment of an individual’s Divine Gift rather than their actions, character, or intent. Institutions, including academies, councils, and religious orders, may unconsciously reinforce this bias through policy, tradition, or selective trust.
It is important to note that Astraea has never defined, endorsed, or acknowledged these categories. The goddess remains silent, and no verifiable doctrine exists to justify the hierarchy imposed upon Gifts. As such, the power struggle surrounding Divine Gifts reflects societal fear, aspiration, and misunderstanding rather than divine will.

Among informed scholars and within certain internal records, this stratification is recognized not as a natural order, but as a flawed interpretive framework imposed by mortals seeking predictability in a system never meant to be judged by visibility or utility.
