At its current stage, SPINE can support a maximum of **10 active clones** at one time. This is a firm hardware limitation—not a safety buffer, soft cap, or administrative setting. The system’s infrastructure simply cannot handle more than 10 live neural stems being synchronized simultaneously without risking catastrophic overload or instability.

The limitation stems from the way SPINE handles real-time data traffic, memory updates, and individual processing demands for each clone. Every active stem (clone) contributes to and draws from the central processing hub, and the system must maintain total consistency across all connected minds with zero latency. Once the 10-clone threshold is reached, any attempt to activate an additional stem would either be rejected or crash the network entirely.

This constraint plays a major role in clone dynamics. It means that whenever a new clone is created or brought online, one of three things must have happened:

1. A previous clone has died.
2. A clone has been voluntarily deactivated.
3. A clone has been disconnected and stored offline.

The result is a deeply felt weight behind each activation. Clones are hyper-aware that space on the network is limited, and every position within SPINE is precious. Death or disconnection isn’t just a loss—it’s a rebalancing of memory flow and emotional presence within the system.

Although there may be plans or aspirations to upgrade SPINE in the future to handle more connections, that capability does not currently exist. All lab protocols, scheduling, and mission planning must work around the 10-clone cap as a fixed law of the system.
